INNOVATION · STEM + VIRTUAL REALITY

A STEM virtual-reality lab for places far from the city

Students in remote communities deserve the same wonder of discovery as anyone else. We are designing a portable lab where virtual reality turns biology, physics, engineering and mathematics into experiences students can see, touch and master.

Status: Concept in design. We are seeking funding and technical partners for a pilot. No pilot has started and no results are claimed yet.

Fund the pilotPartner with us

What is in the lab

Portable learning kit

Virtual reality headsets, a rugged teacher tablet and a protective case, built to travel to remote communities.

Solar power

A portable solar power station so the lab runs without relying on the grid.

Offline learning platform

A library of STEM lessons and a teacher dashboard that work without internet and sync when connected.

How it supports learning

Mastery checkpoints

Every experience ends with a check for understanding; students move on only when they have mastered the skill.

Faith and worldview

Creation, design and stewardship connect science and engineering to purpose and service.

Guide-led, never screen-led

A trained Guide frames each session, discusses what students saw and ties it to real-world problems in their community.

Pilot plan

  1. 1

    Needs and safety review

    Confirm needs with the community and set safe-use rules: short, supervised sessions and the manufacturers' age guidance for every device.

  2. 2

    Build the first lessons

    Create a small set of STEM modules aligned to Chanak's U.S. core and to the local curriculum.

  3. 3

    Pilot with one cohort

    Test the kit and lessons in one microschool, with a trained Guide.

  4. 4

    Measure and share

    Evaluate learning outcomes and share what works, open to external evaluation partners.

Student privacy

The platform is designed to work without collecting students' personal data in the cloud. Progress is tracked with student IDs and stored locally until a teacher syncs it.

Fund a first-of-its-kind pilot
