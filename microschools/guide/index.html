Microschool Starter Guide for churches and co-ops

A practical, eight-step checklist for hosting a small-group, mastery-based community microschool. Written by Chanak Foundation as general guidance; adapt it to your community and to the rules where you live.

Ask us about hostingExplore the network

Eight steps from idea to first cohort

1. Clarify purpose and community

Write one sentence describing who the microschool serves and why. Talk with families and church or co-op leaders before choosing a space or a schedule. Start small: one cohort of a few learners is enough to learn from.

2. Choose a safe, suitable space

A classroom, hall or community room with controlled entry, restrooms, good light and room for small-group work. Confirm with the owner or board that the space may be used for daily instruction and put the arrangement in writing.

3. Check the rules that apply to you

Requirements for home education, private schools and facility use differ by state and country. Ask your state education agency, local fire and zoning offices and your insurer what applies. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

4. Protect children

Adopt a written safeguarding policy: background checks for every adult, two-adult supervision, clear pick-up rules, and a way for families to raise concerns. Keep student records private and never post identifying information about children.

5. Prepare the Guide

A Guide facilitates rather than lectures. Plan training in mastery-based teaching, character formation, bilingual support where needed, and how to track each learner's progress without rushing anyone to the next topic.

6. Design the learning week

Group learners by readiness, not only by age. Mix focused instruction, practice at each learner's own pace and project or service time. Move on from a topic only when the learner has shown they understand it.

7. Involve families

Hold an orientation, share a simple weekly update, and agree on how parents can help. Families remain the primary educators; the microschool supports them.

8. Plan for sustainability

List monthly costs (space, materials, training, insurance) and the sources that will cover them: host contributions, family contributions where appropriate, and charitable gifts. Review the plan every quarter and adjust.
